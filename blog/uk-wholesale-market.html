Selling wholesale in the UK: channels, terms and margins

Wholesale Unsal Ltd · November 2026

"Wholesale" in the UK covers very different routes to market. Choosing the right one — and pricing for it — matters more than finding the most buyers.

The main routes

  • Direct to national retail. Selling straight to chains and supermarkets gives volume and visibility, but buyers expect marketing support, promotional funding and tight service levels.
  • Distributors and agents. A distributor holds stock, sells and collects payment; an agent sells on commission while you ship. Both give you an existing book of accounts in exchange for margin.
  • Discount and variety retail. B&M, Home Bargains, The Range, Poundland and similar chains buy in volume, often direct from the factory, and move fast on value-led ranges.
  • Cash & carry and symbol groups. Booker, Bestway, Parfetts and the buying groups serve thousands of convenience and independent stores — strong for impulse categories.
  • Online wholesale platforms. Faire and Ankorstore make it easy to reach independent shops across the UK and Europe with low minimums, at the cost of a platform commission.

Price architecture first

Every channel takes its own margin, so the recommended retail price has to carry the whole chain: factory cost, freight and duty, warehousing, your margin, the distributor's margin, the retailer's margin and VAT. Brands that set a wholesale price without modelling this end up either unprofitable or uncompetitive on shelf. A consistent price ladder also avoids channel conflict — a retailer will not stay listed if the same product is cheaper on your own website or on a marketplace.

Terms and working capital

Large retailers commonly pay on 30 to 60 days, sometimes longer, and may deduct for promotions, damages or late deliveries. Meanwhile the factory wants paying before shipment. The gap between paying for stock and being paid for it is where many growing suppliers run short of cash. Trade credit insurance, invoice finance and careful deposit terms with factories all help — but they need to be in place before the big order arrives.

Logistics expectations

Retail distribution centres run to booked delivery slots, strict pallet and labelling specifications and service-level penalties. Using a 3PL that already delivers into those networks avoids expensive rejections. For smaller independent accounts, efficient pick-and-pack of mixed cartons matters more than full-pallet handling.

Key takeaways

  • Pick channels deliberately; each one changes your price ladder.
  • Model cash flow from factory deposit to retailer payment, including deductions.
  • Retail compliance — slots, labels, pallets — is part of the product.
